# LEGO illustrations in Djot — design record

Books behaviour found here is collected in `process/APPLE_BOOKS_BEHAVIOUR.md`, the reference for reading-system findings.

Started 2026-10-24 from the "Lottie Interaction 7" book, whose page05 draws a LEGO step with three.js from an LDraw model (`process/LEGO_3D_PAGE.md`). The aim is a Djot syntax in which an author describes a step's parts, colours and starting and final positions, instead of writing and packing an LDraw file for every page. Exploratory; design questions are settled one at a time.

## Working shape (proposed, not yet decided)

A step is a `::: lego` div holding the still image (the content and the no-WebGL fallback, following `process/DJOT_3D_MODELS.md`) and a set of `:part:` directives, which the Djot extension's symbol filter already turns into `<span class="part" data-…>` elements for a DOM transform.

```
{step=1}
::: lego

![A red 2×4 brick placed on a green 4×6 plate](step01.png)

:part:{is=3032 color=Green at="0 0 0"}
:part:{is=3001 color=Red at="1 1 1" from="0 0 9"}

:::
```

- `is=`: LDraw part number; the part's own LDraw description can supply callout and alt-text wording.
- `color=`: LDraw colour name (`Red`, `Green`, `Light_Bluish_Grey`), numbers accepted.
- `at=`: final position. `from=`: starting offset from `at=`; a part without it is already in place, and the parts with it are what the thumb moves and what the parts callout counts.
- `turn=`: rotation about the vertical, in degrees.
- `step=` on the div: the step number shown on the page.

Other routes kept for later: a fenced `ldraw` code block carrying raw LDraw text (for pasted models), and `![…](still.png){model="set.mpd" step=7}` for large models built in BrickLink Studio or LeoCAD.

## Decisions

**Positions are in author units, measured from the part's corner (2026-10-24).** Across the grid, units are studs; upward, units are plates, so a brick is 3 high and stacked parts land on whole numbers (plate at 0, a brick on it at 1, a brick on that brick at 4). The anchor is the corner of the part's footprint nearest the grid origin, at the part's base, taken after any `turn=`, so turning a part never moves its anchor. The page script converts to LDraw units (20 LDU per stud, 8 per plate, −Y up) and finds each part's corner from its loaded bounding box, since LDraw puts a part's origin at the centre of its top face. Raw LDraw coordinates were declined: they need no conversion, but no author reads them.

**One parts library per book (2026-10-24).** Part geometry comes from a single `.mpd` in the book, packed outside the book with three.js's `packLDrawModel.mjs` from an inventory model: an `.ldr` with one line per part the book uses, colour and position irrelevant. Adding a part to the book means adding a line to the inventory, repacking and re-importing the library. The LDraw colour table ships once instead of on every page. Declined: one packed model per page (page05's current form), which repeats the colour table and shared parts on every page. The page script builds each step as LDraw text from the `:part:` lines and resolves the parts against the library.

**Fixed scale, centred on the finished assembly (2026-10-24).** Every step draws at the same scale (25 px per stud, as page04 and page05 do now), so a part is the same size on every page, as instruction readers expect. The camera centres on the bounding box of all parts at their final positions, within the picture area above the thumb strip; starting positions are not counted, so a part dropping from high above may enter from beyond the page edge. Declined: fitting each step to the page, which never overflows but changes a brick's size from page to page. An oversized model may run off the page; a `scale=` attribute on the step was suggested for that case and is not decided.

Assumed with the framing decision, not discussed: the axes follow `lego_step.py`, with x running to the lower right, y to the lower left and height upward, viewed isometrically from the corner where x and y are largest, so these pages match page04.

**`from=` is an offset from the final position (2026-10-24).** Same units as `at=` (studs across, plates up): `from="0 0 9"` starts the part 9 plates above where it lands, which is page04's drop of 3.6 studs. Moving a part's `at=` leaves its motion intact, and a straight drop needs no shorthand. Declined: `from=` as a second absolute position, which reads literally but has to be kept in step with `at=` by hand.

**Source order is build order (2026-10-24).** When several parts have `from=`, they move one after another in the order their `:part:` lines are written, sharing the thumb's travel evenly. Each move eases in and seats with a short hold before the next starts, as page04's single drop does (seated at frame 330 of 392). Moving several parts at once (a symmetric pair of wheels, say) is left for a later grouping syntax if a page needs it.

**LEGO steps stay where the thumb leaves them (2026-10-24).** On release nothing moves, so a reader can stop partway through a build sequence and study it, and carry on from there with the next drag. A change to `lego3d.js`, which springs back today. The Lottie pages keep springing back, since page02's words are about feel rather than instruction; whether page04, the Lottie LEGO step, should stay put to match was not decided.

**Smaller points taken as proposed (2026-10-24).** The library is `../parts.mpd` unless the chapter's frontmatter names another with `lego-parts:`. A part the step cannot draw (not in the library, an unknown colour, a malformed position) is left out and named in a visible note on the page; the rest of the step still draws. `scale=` is left out until a model needs it.

## Prototype (2026-10-24)

Built in `~/Projects/webgl-book-build`, checked in headless Chrome, not yet in the book.

- `ldraw-src/ldraw/models/inventory.ldr` lists twelve common bricks, plates and a tile; packed to `dist/lego/parts.mpd` (103 KB, 15 KB compressed, of which the colour table is most).
- `book-lego/transformDom.js`: a project DOM transform that renames the div's `step` to `data-step` and sets `data-src` to the library.
- `book-lego/lego3d.js`, reworked: acts on `.lego[data-src]`, reads the `span.part` elements the Djot symbol filter makes, builds the step as LDraw text in place of the library's inventory lines, and parses it with the library's part files in one pass. Each part is tagged by a distinct x position while parsing, since the loader skips a part that fails. Places parts by the corner rule from their bounding boxes, centres the finished assembly at a fixed 25 px per stud, moves the `from=` parts in source order with page04's ease and landing point, groups them in the parts callout with counts, stays put on release and resumes from there on the next drag. Keeps the iOS touch cancelling. The still image, when present, is hidden once the model draws and its alt text labels the drawing; without one, the label is composed from the library's part names ("Step 2: add 2 Yellow Brick 2 x 2, 1 Blue Brick 1 x 2.").
- `entry-lego.js` gained `Group`; `three-lego.js` rebuilt.
- `book-lego/lego.css`: classes renamed from `stage-*` to `lego-*`, plus the notes box.
- `lottie/render-3d/shoot_step.sh <source.dj> <drag[,drag…]> <out.png>` renders a Djot source through the real Djot extension (`djot-render.cjs`) and the transform, then applies synthetic drags. Test sources in `lottie/render-3d/steps/`: `page05.dj`, `tower.dj` (three moving parts, one turned), `broken.dj` (each kind of note).

Choices made while building, to review: a moving part is hidden until its share of the travel begins, so only the part being placed hovers; parts dropping from high above pass behind the parts callout; the composed label is English only; the corner rule uses the bounding box, which suits bricks, plates and tiles but would misplace a part with geometry below its base (a Technic pin).

## page06 and the 1970s handle (2026-10-24, proposal)

page06 builds the first two steps of a 1970s LEGO instruction sheet's armchair (the user's scan, `~/Downloads/lego_instructions.jpg`): two blue 2×4 plates, then two red 1×4 bricks and a red 2×2 brick on them. Source in `lottie/render-3d/steps/page06.dj`.

To fit two steps on one page, a moving part may carry its own `step=`: it starts a new step, and the parts after it belong to that step until the next `step=`. The step number and the parts callout change as that part's move begins, and each step's callout shows only its own parts. The div's `step=` labels the first step. Not discussed before building; it is the smallest change that lets a page follow a printed sheet, and the alternative is one page per step.

The handle is redrawn after the sheet's callouts and arrow: a pale yellow panel with a thin dark rule and a slim solid-headed arrow pointing the way to drag, on every 3D LEGO page. A `.classic` class on the step (`{step=1 .classic}`) gives the page the sheet's off-white paper and pale yellow parts panel; page05 keeps its blue page. The Lottie pages keep their own thumb.

Not matched yet: the sheet's red is an orange red, darker in LDraw's `Red`; the drop shadows read as grey patches on the paper.

**Steps 3 and 4 of the armchair on one page (2026-10-24).** `lottie/render-3d/steps/page07.dj`: steps 1–2 as parts without `from=`, a red 1×4 backrest dropped from above (step 3), and a red 2×2 rising into place under the base with `from="0 0 -4"` (step 4). Moves from below need nothing new: `from=` takes a negative height. Once seated the 2×2 is nearly hidden under the base in the isometric view, as on the printed sheet; the user's view is that seeing it move into the hidden place is what makes it read, so a lower camera angle (tried at 25° and 15°) was declined. A rise of 6 plates started behind the thumb; 4 clears it.

**The instruction view is LEGO's house view, not a 1970s style (research 2026-10-24).** Measured from the screen angles of a part's two horizontal edge directions, which fix the camera for an orthographic drawing (tan of the steeper edge = sin(tilt)/tan(around), tan of the flatter = sin(tilt)·tan(around)). The user's 1970s scan (armchair sheet, page 11): edges at about 37° and 22°, giving tilt ≈ 33°, around ≈ 36°. LEGO Classic 11001 (2019, `product.bi.core.pdf/6262053.pdf` from lego.com), step 1, a 4×4 plate: edges at about 39.5° and 21.3°, giving tilt ≈ 34.5°, around ≈ 34.5°. Both are within the error of measuring a scan, and both differ from true isometric (35.26°, 45°, edges at 30° and 30°) mainly in the turn. Published history (LEGO's own article; drawing done from 1967 to 2003 by Palle Munch's studio in Kolding, then by computer) says nothing about the angle; the measurements are the evidence. One more data point from the 1980s–90s would settle it, but two ends of a 45-year span agreeing is strong. What did change between the eras is the page: off-white paper and a pale yellow, square-cornered parts panel in the 1970s; a pale blue, round-cornered panel and red arrows now.

**Decided (2026-10-24): every 3D LEGO page uses LEGO's view**, `TILT = 34`, `AROUND = 35` in `lego3d.js`, replacing true isometric; the temporary `data-tilt`/`data-around` test hooks are gone. page05 no longer matches page04's isometric drawing; that comparison had served its purpose. `.classic` keeps only the 1970s page (paper and yellow panel).

**Real shadows (2026-10-24).** The flat semi-transparent rectangle under a moving part is replaced by a shadow-mapped light from straight above (`lego3d.js`: `renderer.shadowMap`, a `DirectionalLight` sized to the finished assembly, shadow camera `up` set flat since it looks straight down). Every part receives shadows; only the part being placed casts one, and its shadow strength follows that part's landing, softening while it is high. The fill light was turned down (`FILL = 0.6`, `DROP = 3.2`; 0.45 was a little dark) so the shadow shows; the user finds it a little dark but in keeping with the printed sheet. Seen working in Firefox on the author's Mac; headless Chrome with SwiftShader draws no shadow at all, so the render harness cannot check shadows.

**Turning pages (2026-10-25).** A step marked `.spin` has no moving parts: dragging turns the finished model about the vertical through its centre, 90° over the thumb's full travel, and `spin=` sets another angle (`spin=180`, `spin=-45`). Dragging left brings the near side round to the left; the turn follows the finger with no ease and stays where it is left on release. The lights stay put, so faces brighten and darken as they come round; no part casts a shadow. Fixed scale and framing as for a build step, so a long model can come nearer the page edges partway through. No step number unless `step=` is given, and no parts callout. The handle's arrow is curved, after the sheets' turn-the-model arrow. The label is the still image's alt text, else composed ("A model turning, built from 2 Blue Plate 2 x 4, …"). Building then turning on one page was considered and kept separate for now; source order could carry it later without new syntax (the movers take the first part of the travel, the turn the rest). A `.spin` step with `from=` parts gets a note and no turn. First page: the book's `chair` chapter, page07's end state under `{.spin .classic}`. Built in `lego3d.js` (a turntable group) and the project `transformDom.js` (`spin` → `data-spin`); harness source `lottie/render-3d/steps/chair.dj`.

**Slopes and a spaceman (2026-10-25).** The library gains the 45° slopes 3039 (2×2), 3040b (2×1), 3660b (2×2 inverted) and 3665a (2×1 inverted), and a Classic Space minifig as two submodels built once in the library: `spaceman` (standing) and `spaceman-seated` (legs forward at the hip), each ten parts at LDraw's standard minifig offsets (`ldraw-src/ldraw/models/spaceman.ldr`, `spaceman-seated.ldr`, listed in `inventory.ldr`). An author places a figure with one line, `:part:{is=spaceman-seated color=White at="1 1 1" turn=90}`: torso, arms, hips, legs and helmet take the line's colour (LDraw colour 16), the head and hands stay Yellow and the air tanks Light Grey. The figure faces author +y unless turned; `turn=90` faces it along +x. `lego3d.js` now resolves any `is=` against the library's `parts/` and `models/` files by bare name. A figure's arms, hands and air tanks reach past what it stands on, so the bounding-box corner rule needed fractional positions (the author's standing figure on the chair sat at `at="2.2 0.4 1"`). Fixed the same day with a declared footprint: a submodel may carry `0 !FOOTPRINT w d` (studs, along its own x and z) with its origin at the footprint's centre and base, and `lego3d.js` anchors such a part by that footprint's corner, after any turn, instead of its bounding box; parts without one keep the bounding-box rule. Measured with LDrawLoader: standing, the feet are 2×1 studs, centred 1.25 LDU behind the torso line, soles 72 LDU below the torso top; seated, hips and leg backs are flush 52.75 LDU below it and cover 2×2 studs (hips over one row, legs over the next). The standing figure on the chair is now `at="3 1 1" turn=90`. The seated figure's arms and hands pass through the armrests, as a real figure's would on this chair. The printed head and torso take the library from 103 KB to 379 KB (59 KB compressed). Considered and declined: a `:part:` line for each of the ten minifig parts, which would also need rotation about a horizontal axis for the seated legs.

**`scale=` (2026-10-25).** The book's `josh` chapter spells the name in yellow bricks on the J's grid (letters 4 studs wide with 1-stud strokes, brick-high rows, slopes and inverted slopes for rounded corners; lowercase 9 plates tall against the J's 12, the `h` rising to 12). The word is 19 studs long: about 480 px across at the starting angle and nearly 600 px face-on, against a 320 px page. Narrower lowercase was considered (3 studs) and set aside: the slope-rounded corners lose their symmetry and the word is still about 16 studs. So the deferred `scale=` is now in: `{step=1 .spin scale="0.5"}` draws the page's model at that fraction of the house scale (the parts callout is unaffected). Djot needs the decimal quoted. A value that isn't a number above 0 gets a note and the house scale. The project `transformDom.js` renames `scale` to `data-scale`, as it does `step` and `spin`. The fixed-scale decision still stands for ordinary pages; `scale=` is for a model that cannot fit.

**Letter build pages (2026-10-25).** Each letter of the `josh` and `margie` name pages has its own build page before the name (`josh_j` … `margie_e`). Every letter stands on the same green 4×6 plate, already in place, and keeps its name-page coordinates so letters can be copied between the two kinds of page; the plate goes under it, the letter one stud in from the plate's left edge (the M fills it). The grey joining plates and the minifig head stay on the name pages only. Steps are numbered from 1 on each page and follow the order the letter can physically be built in, worked out from its stud connections, not simply row by row: the `r`'s arm slope hangs from the 1×2 plate on its stem and the 1×1 plate under its tip hangs from the slope, so both rise into place from below after the plate is on; the M's middle hangs from its top slopes, so the inner slopes and then the V's 1×2 brick rise from below. The `s`, `h` and `a` are each two stacks joined only by the base plate. The `g`'s tail tiles go on last. Sources are generated by `lottie/render-3d/make_letters.py` into `steps/letters/`.

**Author-defined models (2026-10-25).** A build page can name what it builds: `{step=1 model=letter-j}`. The model is the parts the page builds (those with `from=`), where they end up; the parts already in place, such as the base plate, are not part of it. Any page then places the model as one piece, `:part:{is=letter-j at="0 -3 0"}`: it is placed by the same corner rule, moves as one when it has `from=`, turns with `turn=`, and counts as one in the parts callout. A model part without `color=` takes the colour of the line that places the model (LDraw's colour 16), and models may use other models. Considered: YAML frontmatter (the family-history pattern), which brings app-written records, native numbers and schema validation, against a second notation for parts; declined because the build page itself can be the definition, so a letter is written once for both its build page and the name page. Storage follows photo-regions: the project `transformDom.js` writes each chapter's models to `SOURCE/data/lego/<idref>.json` when it renders, reads every chapter's record (its own first, then spine order, the first definition of a name winning), and copies the models a step uses into the page as hidden `div.lego-model` data. `lego3d.js` builds a model as a group of its parts, each placed in the model's own grid. The known gap: a record is current only once its chapter has rendered since the model changed, and packaging does not render. First use: the ten letter pages define `letter-j` … `letter-e`, and the `josh` and `margie` name pages place them, rendering pixel-identical to the parts they replace; margie's `i` keeps its own parts, since the name page dots it with a minifig head.

**The lowercase alphabet (2026-10-25).** Five turning display pages, `a-e`, `f-j`, `k-o`, `p-u`, `v-z`, each like the name pages: joined green 4×6 plates (grey 2×8s underneath), `scale="0.37"`. The eight letters already built (a e g h i o r s) are placed as their models, keeping their yellow or red; the eighteen new ones are defined in place and recorded for reuse, which needed one addition: a paragraph of parts can name a model, `{model=letter-b}` on the line before it (the transform renames it `data-model` and records all that paragraph's parts). New letters have explicit colours cycling through classic blue, white and black in alphabetical order; repainting a model from the line that places it was considered and set aside for now. Same grid as the name letters: 4 studs wide unless the shape needs otherwise (f, j, t 3; l 2; m, w 5), x-height 9 plates, ascenders to 15; descenders (j, p, q, y) lie flat in front as tiles like the `g`'s tail, and the `j` is dotted like the letter page's `i`. Revised after the author's review: the `c`'s top end, first a 1×2 brick resting on nothing, is now the `r`'s arm (a slope down to the right with a 1×1 plate under its tip), held to the rounded top-left by a 1×2 plate on top; the `k` is 3 wide, its junction a 1×2 brick. The `v`, `x` and `z` read differently from the rest because they rely on slopes for diagonals; left as they are for now. Sources are generated by `lottie/render-3d/make_alphabet.py` into `steps/alphabet/`.

**A model's main colour, and repainting (2026-10-25).** `color=` on the defining step or paragraph (`{step=1 model=letter-i color=Red}`, `{model=letter-b color=Blue}`) colours the parts that give no `color=` of their own and is the model's main colour. Those parts stay colourless in the record, so a line placing the model with `color=` repaints them, while parts with their own colour keep it: `:part:{is=letter-i color=Blue …}` gives a blue stem and dot over the pale-blue spacer. Without `color=` the model draws in its main colour. Colourless parts inside a model take the nearest colour given above them (the placing line, then that line's paragraph or step, then the model's main colour), which is LDraw's colour 16 and how the library's spaceman already behaves (`is=spaceman color=Blue`: blue suit, yellow face). The record becomes `{"color": …, "parts": […]}`; bare-list records from before still read. Considered for later: a colour map on the placing line (`recolor="Red>Blue White>Black"`) for two-tone models. Converting the letters moved each letter's colour from its parts onto its step or paragraph, accent parts (the `i` and `j` spacers) keeping their own. All seventeen letter, name and alphabet pages render pixel-identical before and after. The check found a name collision: the capital J and M had been `letter-j` and `letter-m`, the same names as the lowercase alphabet's; the capitals are now `capital-j` and `capital-m`. A duplicate name is not yet reported anywhere (the first definition in spine order wins silently).

## Open questions

- Whether page04, the Lottie LEGO step, should also stay put on release.
- The four building choices above.

**Thumbnails and drag direction (2026-10-26).** Apple Books photographs each page for its thumbnail shortly after the page has drawn (measured 8–16 ms on an iPad, 19–131 ms on an older iPhone), and nothing tells a page it is being photographed. So a build step opens on its finished state with the handle hidden, goes back to its start after 250 ms and fades the handle in over 250 ms; a turning page only fades its handle in. Thumbnails in Picklets 2.0 show the finished steps on iPad and iPhone. A build step's drag now runs left to right: right to left was a holdover from the Lottie pages, where the drag ended in a page turn, and here it moves the instructions on instead; the handle starts at the lower left with its arrow pointing right. Turning pages keep right to left, because turning the other way showed the model's back first. The Lottie pages keep their direction.
